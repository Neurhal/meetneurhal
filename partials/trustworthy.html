Runtime Governance for AI-Assisted Work

AI-assisted work can move faster than the environments around it were designed to govern.

As workflows accelerate across systems and teams, the operational context surrounding that work can fragment or disappear while execution is still underway. In many environments, governance is strongest at the beginning and end of a workflow but weakest during execution itself. Ownership shifts, evidence scatters, and reviewers gradually lose visibility into whether work should continue safely or requires intervention.

The operational risk emerges between intent and outcome while work is still moving.

Stale Authority, Scattered Evidence, and Broken Handoffs

AI-assisted work increasingly crosses the boundary between response and execution, and the operational context surrounding that work can become fragmented while execution is still underway. Many failures emerge not at the beginning or end of a workflow, but during delegation, escalation, handoff, or shifting ownership when reviewers lose visibility into whether work should continue safely. The challenge is preserving enough authority, evidence continuity, and recovery context for governance to remain attached to execution while work is still moving.

Stale Authority Concerns

Request Draft Privilege Impact

Stale authority emerges when work changes faster than its approval. A task may begin inside one boundary and gradually expand into broader operational impact while the original permissions and assumptions remain unchanged.

The issue is whether authority still matches the work being performed. As scope shifts, reviewers can lose visibility into when execution crossed into a different level of consequence or when intervention became necessary.

Scattered Evidence

Ticket Log Repo Chat

Scattered evidence appears when the record exists, but cannot support reliable judgment. Intent, approvals, tool activity, and review context can fragment across different systems while execution is still moving, making the operational path behind the work difficult to reconstruct.

A workflow may still reach a technically valid final state while carrying very different operational risk from another workflow that appears identical on the surface. The difference often becomes visible only when the full execution path and surrounding context are examined together.

Broken Handoffs

Team Tool Vendor Review

Broken handoffs occur when AI-assisted work reaches the next system, team, tool, vendor, or reviewer without the context that made the previous step acceptable. The task itself may arrive intact while the authority, evidence, assumptions, or recovery posture attached to it do not.

The risk is that execution continues after the governing context has already fragmented. A workflow may still appear technically valid even after visibility into the original operating conditions has been lost, leaving reviewers unable to determine whether work should continue safely or requires intervention.

Evaluating Runtime Assurance Records
